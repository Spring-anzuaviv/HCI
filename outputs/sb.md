# Five-Part Storyboard Illustration Specification

This English-language production specification contains exactly five major storyboard sections and twenty static frames. Each frame must use the same landscape ratio and a separate caption band, freeze one moment and one system state, and keep people and physical laundry work primary except in the intended UI close-ups. A callout may magnify only content physically present in that moment; it may never introduce a before-state, after-state, or future action.

Use a clear, high-contrast illustration style with practical store lighting, restrained decoration, and large sans-serif UI text. Important status meaning must combine readable wording with an icon, border, shape, or typographic weight rather than color alone. Never show real customer names, phone numbers, addresses, or other personal data, and never depict the phone or tablet controlling a washer or any other real machine.

The pleasant expressions requested in the closing moments are expected illustration outcomes, not validated research results. Recommendations, feasibility results, queue previews, confirmations, and drafted messages are simulated prototype behavior. Human review and explicit confirmation remain visible whenever a consequential change is proposed.

## Character Continuity

Mai Anh is a woman around 20 with a petite build; she has an oval face, black hair tied low, and side-swept bangs. These are the only appearance traits permitted for Mai Anh. Her pose, gaze, and expression may change, but the illustrator must not add any other appearance description.

Hương, the previous worker, is a woman around 30 with an average build, a round face, and shoulder-length black hair. She appears only in Storyboard 1 and must remain visually consistent while removing her apron and leaving the store.

The counter customer is a man around 25 with a tall, slim build, a long face, and short black hair. He appears only in Storyboard 2 and remains the same person throughout that exchange.

The at-home customer in Storyboard 5 is anonymous and should be shown only with the minimal detail needed to distinguish a person receiving a phone notification at home. This person is an illustration convention, not research evidence. The urgent caller in Storyboard 4 is never visually identified and must never appear as a face, portrait, name, number, or identifiable body.

## Assumptions and Simulated Data

All character appearances and the anonymous at-home customer are illustration conventions, not research findings. Happy, confident, reassured, and pleasantly satisfied expressions are expected illustration outcomes requested for the storyboard; they have not been validated through user research.

The handover cards, anonymous IDs, order states, queue positions, feasibility calculation, recommendation, affected-order preview, saved reason, and customer-message states are simulated prototype data. Requested 14:30, estimated 15:00–15:10, suggested 15:15, L-118, L-121, and L-130 are illustrative values rather than operational evidence.

The Washer 2 availability notification is simulated and assumed. The source of machine status is unresolved, and no frame asserts sensor integration, reliable automatic detection, or control of a real washer. Mai Anh must still inspect the physical machine and perform all loading, setup, and starting actions herself.

Queue-changing permission, feasibility thresholds, notification delivery channels, delivery failure handling, and the suitability of the phone and tablet form factors remain unvalidated. The prototype requires explicit human confirmation for consequential queue changes and does not imply production readiness.
